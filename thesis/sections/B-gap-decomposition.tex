\section{Foil 1 and the gap decomposition}\label{app:gap}

\paragraph{The OLS coefficient} At $\cstar$ the A0 coefficient is
$\hat\beta=\StarPtBetaHat$ with interval $[\StarPtBetaLo,\,\StarPtBetaHi]$; at the
min-I point it is $\MinIBetaHat$ with $[\MinIBetaLo,\,\MinIBetaHi]$, and at the
median-I point $\MedIBetaHat$ with $[\MedIBetaLo,\,\MedIBetaHi]$. The scale factor
is $K/N=\SeedShare$. Five statements stay with every report of A0 (Foil~1):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A0 is not an identified estimator of the simulator's $\theta_2$ under the
        actual interference model. It is the whole-village policy estimate implied
        by an analyst who assumes no interference, $(K/N)\hat\beta$.
  \item That reading assumes consistency and no interference, conditional
        exchangeability and positivity given $X$, a correctly specified additive
        constant-effect outcome model, and a sampling basis for HC1. The
        deterministic top-degree assignment does not establish those assumptions.
  \item After scaling, the A0 intervals lie strictly inside $\Theta_{\hm}$ and
        exclude the truth at their own point at all three diagnostic points.
        F-OVERCONF is an assumption-contingent interval-geometry finding and not a
        coverage claim for $\theta_2$ under interference.
  \item Across $\RepsEstimand$ paired panels, about $\UnseededShareMeanBetaPct\%$
        of the gap between $\theta_2$ and $(K/N)$ times the mean OLS coefficient is
        the policy effect on unseeded people. For the one displayed panel, the
        shares are about $\StarPtUnseededShareDisplayedPct\%$ at $\cstar$,
        $\MinIUnseededShareDisplayedPct\%$ at min-I and
        $\MedIUnseededShareDisplayedPct\%$ at median-I.
  \item The rescaling by $K/N$ was adopted after the frozen numbers were
        inspected. Its justification is the aggregation identity that holds under
        no interference. The post hoc design correction is disclosed
        (Appendix~\ref{app:disclosures}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The decomposition} With paired streams, the policy effect splits
exactly into a seeded part and an unseeded part. Let $H$ be the seeded set and
$y_2-y_0$ the effect on one person in one replication. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap}
  \theta_2
  = \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{i\in H}(y_2-y_0)}_{\text{seeded part}}
  + \underbrace{\tfrac{1}{N}\textstyle\sum_{i\notin H}(y_2-y_0)}_{\text{unseeded part}},
\end{equation}
averaged over replications. The unseeded part is zero under no interference. The
seeded policy contrast is the mean of $y_2-y_0$ over $H$; under interference it is
not the effect of own seeding alone. Table~\ref{tab:gap} gives the numbers. The
gap for the mean coefficient averages over $\RepsEstimand$ panels, whereas the gap
for the displayed coefficient uses the one panel that A0 sees.

Most of the gap is the effect on people who were not seeded, and an analyst who
assumes no interference has no term for it.

\paragraph{Why there is no flexible-regression foil} On the policy arm, seeding is
a deterministic function of degree and the index tie-break. Conditioning on degree
leaves almost no overlap: no seeded person has degree below $\DegreeCut$, no
unseeded person has degree above it, and only one unseeded person has degree
exactly $\DegreeCut$. Omitting degree leaves the assignment variable unadjusted. A
forest can differ numerically from OLS, but it keeps the same blind spot to
interference and does not identify $\theta_2$ from this panel.
